# Why piBrick Isn't Crowdfunding

**Why not launch a Kickstarter or Indiegogo campaign? It's a fair question.**

Whether it's the original **piBrick PocketCM5** or the newer **piBrick Slide**, crowdfunding might seem like the obvious way to bring these projects to more people.

It can help fund production, measure demand, and generate excitement around a new product.

But I've deliberately chosen a different path for piBrick.

This isn't a statement against crowdfunding. It's a decision based on what I've seen, what I've learned while developing and manufacturing piBrick, and what I believe is the right approach for this project in the long run.

## 1. Too Many Crowdfunded Hardware Projects Never Fulfill Their Promises

We've seen too many crowdfunded handheld and hardware projects struggle to deliver what they promised.

Some face years of delays, some encounter unexpected technical problems, and others never reach their backers at all.

Every project has its own circumstances, and manufacturing hardware is genuinely difficult. But from a buyer's perspective, the result is often the same: money is paid upfront, expectations are set, and uncertainty follows.

I don't want piBrick to become another project that people have to worry about.

## 2. Manufacturing Is Much Harder Than It Looks

Getting a prototype to work is one thing. Producing hundreds or thousands of reliable units is an entirely different challenge.

There are PCBs, component sourcing, assembly, mechanical tolerances, quality control, packaging, shipping, customs, and after-sales support. A small issue in any of these areas can affect the entire production process.

It's easy to underestimate these challenges before committing to a large crowdfunding campaign.

I've experienced these manufacturing challenges firsthand while developing and producing piBrick PocketCM5. Even with relatively small production batches, there are always unexpected details to solve.

With piBrick Slide, I want to apply those lessons rather than take on commitments that exceed what I can realistically deliver.

I'd rather grow production gradually, learn from each batch, and improve the process before making bigger commitments.

## 3. piBrick Should Feel Like a DIY Project

I want piBrick to stay close to its maker roots.

Whether it's PocketCM5 or Slide, piBrick isn't meant to be just another finished consumer gadget that you buy, unbox, and forget about.

It's an open hardware project for people who enjoy building, experimenting, modifying, and understanding how their devices work.

I want users to have the freedom to source components, assemble their devices, explore the hardware, and contribute ideas or improvements.

A more gradual, maker-oriented release gives people room to participate in the process. It also keeps the project focused on hardware openness and community involvement rather than simply maximizing sales.

I believe this approach fits the spirit of piBrick much better than presenting it as a mass-market product from day one.

## 4. I'm Not Willing to Gamble With the piBrick Reputation

This is probably the most important reason.

Building a brand takes time, but losing people's trust can happen very quickly.

The piBrick community and the trust of its users are more important to me than maximizing the number of orders in a single campaign.

A crowdfunding campaign creates expectations and obligations that must be fulfilled, often at a scale that is difficult to predict. I don't want to make promises based on optimistic production estimates and risk disappointing the people who support the project.

I'd rather make smaller commitments that I can realistically deliver.

## So, What's the Alternative?

Instead of crowdfunding, piBrick will continue to develop and release hardware in smaller, manageable batches.

This approach applies to both existing products like PocketCM5 and new projects like Slide.

It means:

- Production can grow according to actual manufacturing capacity.
- Hardware and design issues can be addressed between batches.
- Availability can remain more realistic and transparent.
- The project can evolve with feedback from actual users and makers.
- I can avoid taking on more orders than I can responsibly fulfill.

It may mean slower growth, limited availability, and occasional waiting periods. But I'd rather be honest about those limitations than promise something I cannot guarantee.

## Building Trust, One Batch at a Time

piBrick is an independent open hardware project, and I want to keep it that way: open, experimental, and driven by the maker community.

Not crowdfunding doesn't mean there is no ambition. It simply means choosing a pace that allows the entire piBrick product family to grow without putting its users, supporters, or reputation at unnecessary risk.

I'd rather have a smaller number of happy piBrick owners than thousands of disappointed backers.

**Build carefully. Ship responsibly. Keep it open.**

That's the direction I want piBrick to take.
