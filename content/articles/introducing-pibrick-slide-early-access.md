# Introducing piBrick Slide — Early Access for Makers and Early Adopters

**A new form factor for piBrick is taking shape.**

Today, I'm opening **piBrick Slide** to a small group of early testers and adopters.

piBrick Slide is an experimental handheld Linux computer built around the same **piBrick PocketCM5 MainBoard**, but with a different approach to its physical design: a sliding form factor paired with a **5.48-inch AMOLED display running at 60Hz**.

The goal is to explore a more flexible handheld experience while keeping the openness and hardware foundation that define the piBrick project.

## A familiar foundation, a new design

Rather than starting from scratch, piBrick Slide reuses the PocketCM5 MainBoard. This keeps the core hardware platform consistent while allowing the enclosure, display arrangement, and overall user experience to evolve in a new direction.

The sliding mechanism is the main design feature. It gives the device a different physical interaction compared with the original PocketCM5 and opens up new possibilities for a compact handheld computer.

## Why Early Access?

piBrick Slide is still under development. Before moving further, I want to involve people who are interested in trying experimental hardware and helping shape the project.

This early access phase is intended for:

- Makers and hardware enthusiasts who enjoy testing new devices.
- Existing piBrick users interested in trying the Slide design.
- Linux handheld enthusiasts willing to experiment and report issues.
- People who can provide constructive feedback on usability, hardware, and design.

This is not a finished consumer product or a mass-production release. Some aspects may still change, and the experience may not be as polished as the existing PocketCM5.

## What to expect

Early adopters should expect an actively developing project. Hardware, firmware, software support, and mechanical details may receive updates as testing progresses.

Feedback from this group will help identify issues, validate design decisions, and prioritize improvements before any wider release.

If you're joining the early access phase, please be prepared to share your experience and report problems. Even small observations can be valuable during development.
